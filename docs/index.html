01 / The project

Wild lives, shared landscapes.

A data-led look at how a native predator moves through a landscape shaped by both nature and people.

“Every GPS point is a small clue about how wildlife navigates a changing world.”

This project investigates caracal GPS observations in and around Cape Town, asking how movement relates to road proximity, landscape context and day–night patterns.

A reproducible Python pipeline processes tracking data and generates analyses and visualisations. The aim is to make the analytical process transparent—from data preparation to outputs that can be explored and interpreted.

02 / Research questions

Three questions to investigate.

The analysis focuses on movement speed and whether its relationship with roads varies across place and time.

QUESTION / 01

Do roads matter?

Does caracal movement speed vary with distance from roads?

QUESTION / 02

Does landscape change the pattern?

Does the relationship between road distance and movement differ between urban areas and wildland?

QUESTION / 03

Does time of day matter?

Does the relationship between road distance and movement change between day and night?
